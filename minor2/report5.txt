General Consultation
Patient: Emily Johnson, Age: 55
Date: 03/06/2025
Emily Johnson, a 55-year-old female with a history of osteoarthritis and mild hypertension, came to
the clinic complaining of persistent joint pain and stiffness primarily affecting her knees and lower
back. Emily described the pain as a constant, dull ache that had progressively worsened over several
weeks. She also reported intermittent swelling and a noticeable decrease in her range of motion,
which was affecting her daily activities.
During the consultation, the physician performed an in-depth musculoskeletal examination. Findings
included noticeable crepitus in the knee joints and limited lumbar flexibility. An X-ray of the knees
and lumbar spine was ordered and revealed early osteoarthritic changes, such as joint space
narrowing and the presence of small osteophytes. To rule out inflammatory arthritis, blood tests for
markers such as ESR and CRP were also conducted; these returned within normal limits, supporting a
diagnosis of osteoarthritis.
The management plan for Emily was multifaceted. She was referred to physiotherapy for targeted
exercises to improve joint mobility and strength and was prescribed non-steroidal anti-inflammatory
drugs (NSAIDs) for pain management. The physician also emphasized the importance of weight
management to reduce stress on her joints and provided educational materials on self-care
strategies for chronic joint pain. A follow-up appointment was scheduled for four weeks to monitor
her response to the treatment and make any necessary adjustments to her management plan.
