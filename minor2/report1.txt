General Consultation
Patient Sarah Matthews, Age 42
Date 03082025
Sarah Matthews, a 42-year-old female with a history of seasonal allergies, presented to the clinic
with a persistent dry cough, low-grade fever, and mild fatigue for approximately three days. She
described her cough as non-productive, accompanied by a fluctuating temperature around 99.5°F. In
addition, she mentioned experiencing mild shortness of breath during light activities, though she
denied chest pain or significant respiratory distress.
During the consultation, the physician performed a thorough examination. Lung auscultation
revealed mild bilateral wheezing, especially in the lower lung fields, while oxygen saturation
remained stable at 96% on room air. Given her family history of asthma and her allergy background,
the doctor considered both infectious and allergic etiologies. The patient’s medical history was
reviewed, noting that Sarah had previously managed seasonal allergies with antihistamines but had
not experienced such respiratory symptoms before.
The treatment plan included a short course of a bronchodilator alongside a mild antibiotic therapy to
cover potential bacterial superinfection. Sarah was also advised to increase her fluid intake, rest, and
maintain a symptom diary to document any changes. The physician scheduled a follow-up visit in one
week and discussed the possibility of a chest X-ray if her symptoms persisted or escalated.
