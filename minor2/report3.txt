General Consultation
Patient: Amanda Rodriguez, Age: 30
Date: 03/07/2025
Amanda Rodriguez, a 30-year-old female with no significant prior medical history, visited the clinic
with complaints of intermittent lower abdominal pain and episodes of nausea over the past two
days. She described the pain as cramping and sometimes sharp, primarily localized in the lower
abdomen with occasional radiation toward the pelvic area. Amanda noted that the pain seemed to
intensify after meals, although no specific dietary trigger was identified.
A comprehensive physical examination was performed, with the doctor noting mild tenderness upon
palpation of the lower abdominal quadrants, particularly in the right lower quadrant. There were no
signs of guarding, rebound tenderness, or rigidity, which helped reduce the likelihood of an acute
surgical emergency. Nevertheless, the possibility of appendicitis or gynecological causes, such as
ovarian cysts, was considered. Vital signs were stable, and initial blood work, including a CBC and
CRP, was ordered to evaluate for any underlying inflammatory process.
To further investigate, an abdominal ultrasound was recommended to rule out appendicitis and
assess for other pelvic abnormalities. The physician also encouraged Amanda to keep a detailed
symptom diary, recording the intensity, frequency, and duration of her pain, along with any
associated symptoms such as changes in bowel habits or fever. She was advised to follow a light diet,
take over-the-counter pain relievers if necessary, and seek immediate care should her symptoms
worsen.
